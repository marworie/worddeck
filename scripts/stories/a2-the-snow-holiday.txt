Every winter, Maria's family went to the same place for their holiday: a small hotel by the sea. It was quiet and warm, and her parents loved it. But this year, Maria wanted something different.

"Please," she said at dinner one evening in November. "This year, can we go to the mountains? I want to see real snow. I want to learn to ski."

Maria was fifteen, and she lived in a city in the south of Spain. In her city, it almost never snowed. She saw snow only in films and in photos on the internet.

Her father looked at her mother. Her mother looked at her father.

"The mountains are expensive," said her father.

"And cold," said her mother.

"And we cannot ski," said her younger brother, Pablo. He was ten.

"We can learn!" said Maria. "There are ski schools for beginners. I checked on the internet. And I found a small family hotel in the mountains. It is not very expensive."

She showed them the website on her phone. There were photos of a wooden hotel with a red roof, white mountains and blue sky. Her mother looked at the photos for a long time.

"It is very beautiful," she said finally.

Two days later, her father booked the hotel for one week in January.

The journey was long. First they took a train to Madrid, and then another train to the north. Then they took a bus up into the mountains. The road was narrow and it went up and up and up. Pablo felt a little sick. Maria looked out of the window all the time. At first there were only brown hills and green trees. Then, slowly, the colour changed. There was a little white on the ground. Then more. Then everything was white.

"Snow!" Maria shouted. "Look! Real snow!"

Everybody on the bus laughed, but Maria did not care. She was too happy.

The hotel was exactly like the photos. The owner, a friendly woman called Carmen, met them at the door. Inside it was warm, and there was a big fire in the living room. Their room had two beds and a big window. From the window they could see the mountains and the ski lifts.

"The ski school starts tomorrow at nine o'clock," said Carmen. "You can rent skis, boots and helmets in the shop next door. And don't forget warm gloves. It is very cold up there."

That evening, Maria and Pablo went outside and played in the snow. It was softer than Maria expected, and much colder. They made a small snowman with stones for eyes and a stick for a nose. Then Pablo threw a snowball at Maria, and she threw one back. Soon they were both wet and cold, and they were laughing.

The next morning, the whole family went to the ski school. Their teacher was a young man called Iker. He was very patient and he had a big smile.

"Today you will learn three things," he said. "How to stand on your skis. How to walk with them. And, most important, how to stop."

It sounded easy. It was not easy.

The skis were long and heavy, and the boots were very hard. When Maria tried to stand, her skis went in different directions and she fell into the snow. Pablo fell too. Their father fell three times in one minute. Only their mother did not fall, and everybody was surprised.

"I am a natural!" she said, and then she fell too.

Iker helped them up again and again. "Everybody falls," he said. "Falling is part of learning. The important thing is to get up."

By the end of the first day, Maria could stand on her skis and move slowly down a very small hill. She could also stop, most of the time. Her legs hurt and her face was red from the cold, but she felt fantastic.

The days went quickly. Every morning they had ski lessons, and every afternoon they practised. Pablo learned very fast. By the third day, he was going down the small hill without falling. Maria was slower, but she was careful. Their father spent a lot of time on the ground, but he always got up and tried again.

On the fourth day, there was a big storm. The wind was very strong and it snowed all day. The ski lifts were closed. Nobody could go outside.

At first, Maria was disappointed. But it turned out to be one of the best days of the holiday. Carmen made hot chocolate for everybody in the hotel. The guests sat by the fire and played cards and board games. An old man from Germany told funny stories about his trips around the world. A family from France taught Maria and Pablo a new card game. In the evening, Carmen's husband played the guitar and everyone sang.

"I did not know you could sing," Maria said to her father.

"I did not know either," he said, and they laughed.

On the fifth day, the storm stopped. The sky was perfectly blue, and there was fresh snow everywhere. Iker had a surprise.

"Today," he said, "we will take the big lift. We will go to the top of the mountain, and you will ski down the green slope. It is the easiest slope, but it is long. Are you ready?"

Maria was nervous. The lift went very high. From the top, she could see mountains in every direction, white and silent under the blue sky. It was the most beautiful thing she had ever seen.

"Wow," said Pablo quietly. For once, he had nothing else to say.

The slope looked very long from the top. Maria took a deep breath and started to move. She went slowly at first, turning left and right like Iker taught them. Then a little faster. She fell once, near the middle, but she got up quickly and continued. The cold air was on her face, and the snow was shining in the sun. When she finally arrived at the bottom, she raised her arms in the air.

"I did it!" she shouted.

Her mother and Pablo arrived soon after her. Her father came last, a little slowly, but he did not fall once. Iker gave everybody a high five.

On their last evening, Carmen made a special dinner. Maria sat by the window and looked at the mountains in the moonlight.

"So," said her mother. "Next winter. The sea or the mountains?"

Maria smiled. Pablo smiled. Even her father smiled.

"The mountains," they all said together.
