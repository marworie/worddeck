Emma woke up at six o'clock, one hour before her alarm. She could not sleep any more. Today was a very important day. It was her first day at her first real job.

Emma was twenty-two. She finished university in June with a degree in design. For three months she looked for a job. She sent her CV to more than forty companies. Most of them never answered. Some of them said, "Sorry, we need someone with more experience." She had two interviews, but she did not get those jobs. She started to feel very worried.

Then, in September, a small company called Blue Bird Studio invited her to an interview. They made websites and apps for small businesses. The interview went well. The manager, a woman called Ruth, asked her many questions and looked carefully at her work. One week later, Ruth called her.

"We would like to offer you the job," she said. "Can you start on Monday?"

Emma said yes so fast that Ruth laughed.

Now it was Monday. Emma took a shower and got dressed. She tried three different outfits before she chose one: dark blue trousers, a white shirt and comfortable shoes. She did not know what people wore at the studio. She did not want to look too formal or too casual. She made coffee, but she was too nervous to eat breakfast.

Her flatmate, Jess, came into the kitchen in her pyjamas.

"Big day!" said Jess. "How do you feel?"

"Terrible," said Emma. "What if they do not like me? What if I make a mistake? What if I do not understand anything?"

Jess put her hands on Emma's shoulders. "Listen. They chose you. Out of all the people, they chose you. Nobody expects you to know everything on your first day. Just be yourself, ask questions and try to remember people's names."

Emma took a deep breath. "Okay. Ask questions. Remember names. Okay."

The studio was in an old building near the city centre. Emma arrived twenty minutes early, so she walked around the block twice. At five to nine, she went in.

The office was not what she expected. It was one big room with large windows, many plants and a long wooden table in the middle. There was a small kitchen in the corner and a sofa near the window. About ten people were working there. Some were sitting at desks with two computer screens. Two people were standing in front of a whiteboard, talking and drawing. Nobody was wearing a white shirt. Most people wore jeans and T-shirts.

Ruth came to meet her with a big smile. "Emma! Welcome! Come in. Let me introduce you to everyone."

Emma tried hard to remember the names. There was Tom, a developer with a long beard. There was Priya, the other designer, who had bright green glasses. There was Marco, who was responsible for clients and talked very fast. There was Ana, who managed the projects. And there was a big brown dog under one of the desks.

"And that is Biscuit," said Ruth. "He belongs to Tom. He comes to work every Tuesday and Friday."

"But today is Monday," said Emma.

"Biscuit does not know that," said Tom, and everybody laughed.

Ruth showed Emma her desk. It was next to Priya's. There was a new laptop, a big screen and a small plant. On the desk there was also a card. It said: "Welcome to the team, Emma!" Everybody had signed it.

Emma felt her shoulders relax a little.

The morning was busy. First, Ana explained how the company worked: how they talked to clients, how they planned projects and how they shared files. There were many new words and many new programs. Emma wrote everything in a notebook. Then Priya showed her the project she was going to help with: a new website for a small bakery in the city.

"The owner is a lovely old man," Priya said. "He has had the bakery for forty years. He wants a website, but he does not want it to look too modern. He wants it to feel warm and friendly, like his shop."

"Can I see photos of the shop?" asked Emma.

Priya looked surprised and pleased. "Good question! Yes, here they are."

They looked at the photos together. The bakery had old wooden shelves, yellow walls and a big window full of bread and cakes. Emma started to have ideas immediately.

At one o'clock, everybody stopped working and had lunch together at the long table. Some people brought food from home. Others bought sandwiches from the cafe across the street. They talked about films, holidays and a terrible restaurant that Marco went to on Saturday. Nobody talked about work. Emma listened and laughed and, for the first time that day, she did not feel like a stranger.

In the afternoon, Emma made her first mistake. She was working on a file for the bakery website, and she accidentally deleted a folder. She did not know what to do. Her face became hot. For a few minutes she sat very still and looked at the screen. Then she remembered what Jess said: ask questions.

She turned to Priya. "I am so sorry. I think I deleted something important."

Priya looked at the screen. "Oh, that? Don't worry. Everything is saved automatically. Look." She clicked twice, and the folder came back. "Everybody does that in their first week. I did it on my first day too. Actually, I did it on my first day, my second day and my fifth day."

Emma laughed with relief.

At half past five, people started to go home. Ruth came to Emma's desk.

"So," she said. "How was your first day?"

Emma thought about it. "It was a lot," she said honestly. "A lot of new names, new programs and new information. But everyone is very kind. And I already have some ideas for the bakery."

"That is exactly how a first day should be," said Ruth. "Go home and rest. See you tomorrow."

When Emma got home, Jess was waiting for her with two cups of tea.

"Well?" said Jess. "Tell me everything."

Emma sat down on the sofa. "There is a dog called Biscuit," she said. "I deleted a folder. And I think I am going to love this job."

That night, Emma slept very well.
