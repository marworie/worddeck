Daniel was nineteen years old and he was a student at the university. He studied computer science, and he loved technology. He had a new phone, and it was the most important thing in his life. He used it for everything: for messages, for music, for maps, for photos and for his bank. He never went anywhere without it.

One Friday evening, Daniel took the bus home from the university. The bus was full, so he stood near the back door. He was very tired after a long week of exams. He listened to music on his phone and looked out of the window at the city lights.

When the bus arrived at his stop, Daniel got off quickly. He walked to his building, went up the stairs and opened the door of his small flat. Then he put his hand in his pocket.

His phone was not there.

He checked his other pocket. He checked his bag. He checked his jacket again. Nothing. His heart started to beat very fast.

"No, no, no," he said. "This is not possible."

He tried to remember. He had the phone on the bus. He was listening to music. Then the bus stopped and he got off. Maybe the phone fell out of his pocket when he got off. Or maybe it was still on the bus.

Daniel ran down the stairs and back to the bus stop. Of course, the bus was not there. He looked on the ground near the stop. He looked under the bench. He found an old ticket and a sweet paper, but no phone.

He went back to his flat and sat on the sofa. He felt terrible. He could not call anyone, because he did not know any phone numbers. All the numbers were in his phone. He could not send a message to his friends. He could not even check the time, because he did not have a clock. He looked around his flat and realised something strange: without his phone, he did not know what to do.

He had an old laptop, so he opened it. He found the website of the city bus company. There was a page called "Lost and Found". It said: "If you lost something on a bus, please visit our office at the central station. The office is open from Monday to Saturday, from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m."

"Tomorrow is Saturday," Daniel said. "Okay. I can go tomorrow."

That night, Daniel did not sleep very well. He woke up three times and reached for his phone, but it was not there. In the morning, he did not know the time, so he looked out of the window. The sun was up, and people were walking in the street. He got dressed quickly and went to the central station.

The central station was big and busy. There were trains, buses and many people with suitcases. Daniel did not have a map on his phone, so he had to ask for directions. This was new for him. He usually never asked people for anything.

He saw an old man selling newspapers. "Excuse me," Daniel said. "Do you know where the Lost and Found office is?"

The old man smiled. "Of course. Go straight, past the coffee shop, then turn left. It is next to the ticket machines. You lost something?"

"My phone," said Daniel.

"Ah," said the old man. "Young people and their phones. Good luck!"

Daniel followed the directions and found the office. There was a short line of people. A woman in front of him was looking for her umbrella. A man behind him was looking for a black bag. When it was his turn, Daniel walked to the desk. A young woman with glasses was working there.

"Good morning. How can I help you?" she asked.

"I lost my phone on the bus yesterday evening," Daniel said. "Bus number 42. At about seven o'clock."

"Can you describe it?" she asked.

"It is black, and it has a blue case. There is a small sticker on the back. It is a picture of a cat."

The woman typed something on her computer. Then she went into a back room. Daniel waited. He could not stop moving his feet. After a few minutes, the woman came back. She had a small box in her hands.

"We have three phones from bus 42 yesterday," she said. "Is one of them yours?"

She opened the box. Daniel looked inside. There was a white phone, a red phone and a black phone with a blue case. On the back, there was a small sticker of a cat.

"That one!" Daniel said. "That is my phone!"

"Can you open it, please? I need to check that it is really yours."

Daniel took the phone and put in his code. The phone opened. On the screen, there was a photo of Daniel with his family at the beach.

"Perfect," said the woman. "A bus driver found it under a seat at the end of his shift. You are lucky. Not all lost phones come back."

"Thank you so much," Daniel said. "Really, thank you."

He signed a paper and walked out of the office with his phone in his hand. He turned it on. There were twenty-three messages and seven missed calls. His mother called four times. His best friend, Leo, sent eleven messages. The last one said: "Are you alive??"

Daniel laughed and called his mother first. She was worried, but she was happy to hear his voice. Then he sent a message to Leo: "I am alive. I lost my phone. Long story. Coffee later?"

On the way home, Daniel walked past the old man with the newspapers.

"Did you find it?" the old man asked.

"Yes!" said Daniel, and he showed him the phone.

"Good," said the old man. "But tell me, young man. What did you do last night, without your phone?"

Daniel thought for a moment. "Nothing," he said. "I did not know what to do. It was a little scary."

The old man nodded. "When I was young, we did not have phones. We read books. We talked to people. We looked at the sky. You should try it sometimes. Not every day. But sometimes."

Daniel thought about these words all the way home. That evening, he met Leo at a cafe. He told him the whole story. Then he did something unusual. He put his phone in his bag, and he did not take it out for two hours. They talked, they laughed, and they watched the people in the street.

It was one of the best evenings Daniel had in a long time.

Now Daniel still loves his phone, and he still uses it every day. But on Sunday mornings, he turns it off for a few hours. He reads a book, he walks in the park, or he visits his grandmother. And he always checks his pocket before he gets off the bus.
